Theoretical Foundations of SGP4/SDP4 Constellation Propagation & Spherical Graticule Projection

1. Analytical Perturbation Theory: SGP4 and SDP4 Formulations

Unlike idealized two-body Keplerian motion—which assumes a spherically symmetric central gravitational potential Φ(r) = -μ/r—artificial Earth satellites experience secular and periodic orbital perturbations. The Simplified General Perturbations Model 4 (SGP4) integrates Brouwer’s gravitational theory with non-conservative atmospheric drag power density functions to propagate near-Earth objects (orbital period T < 225 min). For deep-space regimes (T ≥ 225 min, such as geostationary Bell Nimiq, Telus Anik, and MEO GLONASS nodes), SDP4 incorporates lunisolar gravitational harmonics and Earth tesseral resonance terms (J2,2).

2. Reference Frame Transformations: TEME → ECEF → WGS-84 Geodetic

Two-Line Element (TLE) state vectors are natively parameterized in the True Equator, Mean Equinox (TEME) quasi-inertial coordinate frame. To map each spacecraft onto our interactive rotating spherical graticule, the system computes the Greenwich Mean Sidereal Time (θGMST) at each 11-second telemetry epoch and applies an axial rotation matrix R3(θGMST) to transform position vectors into the Earth-Centered, Earth-Fixed (ECEF) frame, followed by Bowring’s iterative ellipsoidal conversion into WGS-84 geodetic latitude (φ), longitude (λ), and orthometric altitude (h).

3. Multi-Constellation Architectural Diversity

Modern orbital infrastructure spans distinct dynamical shells: low-latency Ku/Ka-band mega-constellations (Starlink / Starlynx, Huwiea LEO Relay, GodioUSA) operating at 525–610 km inclinations of 53°–97.6°; scientific Earth-observing sun-synchronous platforms (NASA Terra, Aqua, Landsat 9); high-inclination navigation and tactical relays (Russian GLONASS & Nostrous); and Clarke-Belt geostationary broadcast transponders (Telus Anik, Rogers-Sat, and Bell Nimiq at 35,786 km).
